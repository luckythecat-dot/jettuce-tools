Topic Validation System

Build a stack of evidence to avoid spending 30 hours on a dead topic.

IDEA ➔ DEMAND ➔ COMPETITION ➔ GAP ➔ PACKAGING ➔ FEASIBILITY ➔ DECISION

0 First: The One-Sentence Rule

Before opening any tools, force yourself to articulate your premise.

Formula:
"I want to make a video about [THING] because [REASON PEOPLE MIGHT CARE]."
  • ✅
    Good: "I want to make a video about O2Jam because it was a huge part of Filipino internet-café culture and I want to understand what happened to it."
  • ✅
    Good: "I want to make a video about Insaniquarium because it is bizarre enough that the experience of playing it might itself be entertaining."
  • ❌
    Bad: If you can't explain why a stranger would care, do not research it yet.

Tool Signals

Interviewing the search bar for demand and context.

1 YT Autocomplete

Your first cheap signal. Start typing the entity and see what branches out.

O2Jam why
O2Jam history
what happened to O2Jam

Good: Multiple questions surround the entity.
Less Interesting: You type the game's name and nothing useful appears.

2 Google Autocomplete

Reflects actual searches, location context, and trending interest. Ask it questions:

  • Problem: why [entity]
  • History: [entity] history
  • Culture: [entity] Philippines
  • Current: [entity] 2026

3 Google Trends

Search as a Topic (not just search term) to gauge broad interest. Test timelines: 5 years ➔ 12 months ➔ 90 days.

🟢 Evergreen

Continuous interest. Great for "Why Was X So Addictive?"

🟢 Resurging

Low baseline, sudden spike. Great for "Why is everyone playing X again?"

🔴 Dead

Flatlines forever. Idea needs to be exceptionally compelling.

4 Compare Entities

Use Trends to benchmark. e.g., Peggle vs PvZ.

If your game is smaller, it means a smaller audience but potentially less competition and a tighter community.

5 vidIQ Keyword

Find the language the audience uses. They might not search "Retrospective", but they do search "why is it addictive".

Competition Audit

Finding out what already exists and where the gaps are.

6 Search the Actual Idea

Stop asking: "Is Peggle popular?"
Start asking: "Does the exact video I want to make already exist?"

7 The Competition Table

Swipe right/left to view all columns.

Video Views Age Topic / Angle Strength Weakness
Video A 2.1M 4y History Strong Authority Old / Outdated
Video B 300K 8mo Addiction Great Premise Weak Thumbnail
Video C 12K 2mo Review Recent Tiny Reach / Weak

8 & 9 Content Gaps

Check YT Studio Trends tab for official gaps. But manually hunt for Angle Gaps.

If there's a 2018 history video and a 2022 documentary, but nobody made "Why Did Everyone in the PH Play O2Jam?" ➔ That's your gap.

11 & 12 Outliers

Look for videos that outperformed their channel's average.

Channel usually gets 20K views, but their video on your topic got 700K. This proves topic appeal outside subscribers.

Packaging & Social

Ensuring the idea is clickable and has human momentum.

13 Thumbnail Audit

What visual promise is the competition making? (Logos, nostalgic screenshots, dramatic faces).

Can you visually communicate a DIFFERENT but equally obvious promise?

14 Write 3-5 Titles

If you can only think of "O2Jam Retrospective", park it.

  • Why Did Everyone in the PH Play O2Jam?
  • Whatever Happened to O2Jam?
  • The Game That Took Over the Philippines

15 The "Stranger Test"

Title A: "O2Jam Retrospective" ➔ Strangers don't care.

Title B: "Why Did Everyone in the Philippines Play O2Jam?" ➔ Strangers are curious about the phenomenon, even if they don't know the game.

16 Reddit / Forums

Look for emotional energy, nostalgia, arguments, and rediscovery. High emotional energy offsets low raw search volume.

17 & 18 TikTok & News

Is the topic experiencing a cultural momentum surge elsewhere? (Viral TikToks, new dev announcements, game shutdowns).

The 5-Pass SOP

Don't spend 3 hours researching. Follow this timed framework.

10 MIN

PASS 1: "Does anyone care?"

Check YT Auto, Google Auto, Trends, vidIQ. Search the obvious title.

Action: If absolutely nothing is happening, park it.

15-20 MIN

PASS 2: "Is there opportunity?"

Audit 10-20 top videos. Record views, age, channel size, angles. Find recent outliers and angle gaps.

10 MIN

PASS 3: "Can I package this?"

Write 5 titles. Sketch 2-3 thumbnail concepts.

Action: If titles suck and thumbs are impossible, park it.

10 MIN

PASS 4: "Is there human interest?"

Check Reddit, TikTok, comments. Look for people talking, not just searching.

5 MIN

PASS 5: "Is this worth MY time?"

1. How many hours will this take? (Remember fatigue)
2. How strong is the evidence?
3. Am I personally excited enough to do it?

Validation Rubric

If you get 5-6 strong signals out of 7, greenlight the project.

Signal Assessment Goal
DemandDo people care?
MomentumDo people care right now?
CompetitionHow many people already made this?
GapIs there an angle they haven't nailed?
PackagingCan I make someone curious in one image/title?
ProductionCan I make this without sacrificing a month?
Personal FitAm I excited enough to make this entertaining?
